FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS — BILLIE JEAN LAW

How much does a consultation cost?
Your first consultation is completely free and lasts 30 minutes. There is no obligation and no pressure. We want you to have the information you need to make a confident decision.

How do personal injury fees work?
Personal injury cases are handled on a contingency fee basis. We only get paid if you win. Our standard contingency fee is one-third (33.3%) of the recovery, plus reimbursement of case expenses such as filing fees, expert witnesses, and medical record retrieval. You pay nothing out of pocket to pursue your case.

How do family law and criminal defense fees work?
These matters are billed at an hourly rate. We provide a clear, written fee agreement before any work begins. We will always give you an honest assessment of your case and your realistic options so you can make an informed decision about whether to proceed.

How much does estate planning cost?
We offer flat-fee estate planning packages. A simple will starts at $350. A comprehensive estate plan — including a will, revocable trust, durable power of attorney, and healthcare directive — starts at $1,200. Exact pricing depends on the complexity of your estate and is quoted at the consultation.

What should I bring to my consultation?
For personal injury: any accident or police reports, medical records or bills you have received, photos of your injuries or the scene, insurance information for all parties involved, and a written timeline of what happened and the treatment you have received.

For family law: your marriage certificate, financial documents including recent pay stubs, last two years of tax returns, and bank and investment account statements. Bring any existing court orders and a list of marital assets (home, vehicles, retirement accounts) and debts.

For criminal defense: your court date, court location, and case number if you have them. Bring all paperwork received from law enforcement or the court and a written account of the events in your own words.

For estate planning: a list of your assets and who you want to receive them, the names and contact information of your intended executor, healthcare proxy, and beneficiaries, and any existing estate planning documents including old wills or powers of attorney.

What is the statute of limitations for my case?
In Virginia:
- Personal injury (car accident, slip and fall, assault): 2 years from date of injury
- Wrongful death: 2 years from date of death
- Medical malpractice: 2 years from injury or discovery (with limited exceptions)
- Property damage: 5 years
- Breach of written contract: 5 years
- Breach of oral contract: 3 years

Missing the statute of limitations almost always permanently bars your claim. If you have any uncertainty about your deadline, contact us immediately.

Do I need an attorney for a simple will?
Virginia wills can be challenged for technical errors, improper witnessing, or lack of testamentary capacity — and the cost of litigation over a defective will far exceeds the cost of proper drafting. For most people, the peace of mind from a properly executed document is worth the modest cost.

What if I cannot afford an attorney?
For personal injury, the contingency fee structure means you pay nothing unless we recover for you. For other matters, we offer payment plans. We will discuss your options at the consultation.

I was in an accident and the other driver's insurance company called me. Should I talk to them?
No. You should not give recorded statements or sign anything presented by an opposing insurance company before consulting an attorney. Insurance adjusters are trained to minimize payouts. A statement you make early — even one that seems harmless — can be used against you later. Call us first.

What geographic areas do you serve?
We primarily serve Fredericksburg City, Stafford County, Spotsylvania County, Caroline County, and the broader Northern Virginia and Rappahannock region. All attorneys are licensed to practice throughout the Commonwealth of Virginia.

How long will my case take?
Timeline depends heavily on case type and whether the matter settles or goes to trial. A straightforward personal injury settlement may resolve in six to twelve months. Contested divorce or complex litigation can take one to three years. We will give you an honest timeline estimate at your consultation — and we will not string you along with false optimism.

Do you handle cases involving Quantico or military-related matters?
Yes. With Quantico nearby, we regularly assist active-duty service members and military families with estate planning, power of attorney documents for deployment, and family law matters. Service members have specific protections under the Servicemembers Civil Relief Act (SCRA) that can affect divorce timelines and financial obligations.

What happens after I schedule a consultation?
A team member from Billie Jean Law will call you within one business hour to confirm your appointment and answer any logistical questions. The consultation itself is 30 minutes with the attorney assigned to your practice area. There is no pressure and no obligation — it is an information session for both sides.
