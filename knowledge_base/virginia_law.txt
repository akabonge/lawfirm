VIRGINIA LAW REFERENCE — BILLIE JEAN LAW KNOWLEDGE BASE

STATUTES OF LIMITATIONS (Virginia Code)

Personal injury: 2 years from date of injury — Va. Code § 8.01-243(A)
Wrongful death: 2 years from date of death — Va. Code § 8.01-244
Medical malpractice: 2 years from date of injury or discovery — Va. Code § 8.01-243(C)
Property damage: 5 years — Va. Code § 8.01-243(B)
Breach of written contract: 5 years — Va. Code § 8.01-246(2)
Breach of oral contract: 3 years — Va. Code § 8.01-246(4)
Fraud: 2 years from discovery — Va. Code § 8.01-243(D)
Libel and slander: 1 year — Va. Code § 8.01-247.1

Note on tolling: The SOL may be tolled (paused) if the plaintiff was a minor at the time of injury, if the defendant fraudulently concealed the cause of action, or in medical malpractice cases where the injury was not discoverable through reasonable diligence.


CONTRIBUTORY NEGLIGENCE (Virginia Rule)

Virginia is one of only four states (along with Alabama, Maryland, and North Carolina, plus Washington D.C.) that follows pure contributory negligence. Under this doctrine, if a plaintiff is found to be even 1% at fault for their own injury, they are completely barred from recovering any damages from the defendant. This is extremely plaintiff-unfavorable compared to comparative negligence states. It makes early legal consultation and evidence preservation critical in personal injury cases.


DUI LAW — Virginia Code § 18.2-266

First offense, BAC 0.08-0.149%: Class 1 misdemeanor. Up to 12 months jail. Fine $250-$2,500. 12-month administrative license suspension. Mandatory ASAP program enrollment.

First offense, BAC 0.15-0.20%: Same as above plus mandatory minimum 5 days jail.

First offense, BAC above 0.20%: Mandatory minimum 10 days jail.

Second offense within 5 years: Mandatory minimum 20 days jail. 3-year license suspension. Ignition interlock required.

Second offense within 10 years: Mandatory minimum 10 days jail.

Felony DUI (third or subsequent offense, or DUI resulting in serious injury/death): Class 6 felony, mandatory minimum 1 year imprisonment.

Implied Consent: Virginia drivers impliedly consent to a blood or breath test when lawfully arrested for DUI. Refusal is a separate civil offense on first refusal, and a Class 1 misdemeanor on subsequent refusals, resulting in 1-year (first) or 3-year (subsequent) license suspension.


RECKLESS DRIVING — Virginia Code § 46.2-862

Driving 20 mph or more over the posted speed limit, or driving at any speed exceeding 85 mph, is a Class 1 misdemeanor in Virginia — regardless of road conditions or traffic. Penalties: up to 12 months jail, fine up to $2,500, 6 demerit points (highest level), potential license suspension. A criminal conviction that appears on background checks. This is frequently charged on I-95 and Route 1 in the Fredericksburg area.


VIRGINIA DIVORCE LAW — Virginia Code § 20-91

Grounds for divorce from the bond of matrimony:
1. Adultery, sodomy, or buggery outside the marriage (immediate grounds; bars adultery-based alimony)
2. Conviction of a felony subsequent to the marriage and confinement for more than 1 year
3. Willful desertion or abandonment for 1 year
4. Cruelty or reasonable apprehension of bodily hurt
5. No-fault: living separate and apart for 1 year (or 6 months with no minor children and a written separation agreement)

Equitable Distribution (Va. Code § 20-107.3): Courts consider: duration of marriage; contributions of each party; circumstances leading to the divorce; how and when property was acquired; debts; tax consequences; and whether either party dissipated marital assets.


CHILD CUSTODY — Virginia Code § 20-124.3

Best interests of the child factors:
1. Age, physical and mental condition of the child
2. Age, physical and mental condition of each parent
3. Relationship between each parent and the child, including the parent's ability to assess and meet the child's emotional, intellectual, and physical needs
4. Needs of the child
5. Role each parent has played and will play in the child's upbringing and care
6. Each parent's propensity to actively support the child's contact with the other parent and to maintain a positive relationship between the child and other parent
7. Reasonable preference of the child (if the court deems the child of reasonable intelligence and understanding)
8. Any history of family abuse as defined in Va. Code § 16.1-228
9. Any history of sexual abuse
10. Such other factors as the court deems necessary


VIRGINIA PROTECTIVE ORDERS — Virginia Code § 16.1-253 et seq.

Emergency Protective Order (EPO): Issued by a magistrate or judge without the respondent present. Duration: up to 72 hours. Can cover no-contact, exclusion from residence, custody.

Preliminary Protective Order (PPO): Issued by General District Court after an ex parte hearing. Duration: up to 15 days (until full hearing). Can include temporary custody provisions.

Permanent Protective Order: Issued after a full hearing with both parties present. Duration: up to 2 years, may be extended. Can prohibit contact, require surrender of firearms (consistent with federal law), assign temporary custody, and exclude respondent from shared residence.


VIRGINIA CHILD SUPPORT — Virginia Code § 20-108.2

Virginia uses the Income Shares Model: child support is based on the combined gross monthly income of both parents and allocated proportionally. Adjustments include work-related childcare costs, health insurance premiums, and extraordinary medical expenses. The schedule is set by statute and provides presumptive amounts that can be deviated from only for good cause shown.

VIRGINIA STATE BAR
