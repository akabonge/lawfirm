PERSONAL INJURY

Billie Jean Law represents injury victims across Fredericksburg, Stafford County, Spotsylvania County, and the surrounding Northern Virginia region.

Car and Truck Accidents
The I-95 corridor through Fredericksburg is one of the most congested and accident-prone stretches on the East Coast. We handle rear-end collisions, intersection accidents, multi-vehicle pileups, drunk driving crashes, distracted driving cases, and commercial truck accidents. Truck cases are especially complex — they often involve multiple defendants including the driver, trucking company, cargo loaders, and maintenance contractors, plus FMCSA regulatory violations and electronic logging device data.

Slip and Fall and Premises Liability
Property owners in Virginia have a legal duty to maintain safe conditions for visitors. If you were injured due to a wet floor, broken steps, inadequate lighting, uneven pavement, or a poorly maintained property, you may have a premises liability claim.

Wrongful Death
When a loved one dies due to someone else's negligence — in a car accident, medical error, or workplace incident — surviving family members may bring a wrongful death claim under Virginia Code § 8.01-50. Recoverable damages include funeral and burial expenses, lost income the deceased would have earned, and loss of companionship and society.

Medical Malpractice
Virginia medical malpractice claims require an expert witness certification before filing. These cases have a two-year statute of limitations and complex procedural requirements. Contact us promptly if you believe a doctor, hospital, or other healthcare provider caused you harm through negligence.

Important Virginia Note
Virginia is one of only four states with pure contributory negligence. If you are found even 1% at fault for your own injury, you may be completely barred from recovering compensation. This makes experienced representation critical — do not speak with insurance adjusters or sign anything before consulting an attorney.


FAMILY LAW

Divorce
Virginia recognizes both no-fault and fault-based divorce. No-fault divorce requires a separation period of six months if you have no minor children and have a written separation agreement, or twelve months if you have minor children or no agreement. Fault grounds include adultery, desertion (one year), conviction of a felony after marriage, and cruelty or reasonable apprehension of bodily harm.

Property Division
Virginia is an equitable distribution state. Courts divide marital property fairly — not necessarily equally — based on statutory factors including the length of the marriage, each spouse's monetary and non-monetary contributions, the circumstances that led to the divorce, and dissipation of assets. Separate property (owned before marriage or inherited) is generally not subject to division.

Child Custody and Visitation
Virginia courts decide custody based on the best interests of the child, weighing ten statutory factors including each parent's relationship with the child, the child's adjustment to home and school, and each parent's willingness to support the other parent's relationship with the child. We handle initial custody orders, modifications due to changed circumstances, and interstate relocation disputes.

Child Support
Virginia calculates child support using the Income Shares Model based on both parents' combined gross monthly income and the custody arrangement. We assist with initial orders, modifications when circumstances change substantially, and enforcement through the courts.

Protective Orders
Virginia courts issue Emergency Protective Orders (EPOs, up to 72 hours), Preliminary Protective Orders (up to 15 days), and Permanent Protective Orders (up to two years) for victims of domestic violence, stalking, or sexual assault. These orders can cover no-contact provisions, custody, residence exclusion, and firearms surrender.


CRIMINAL DEFENSE

DUI and DWI
Virginia has some of the strictest DUI laws in the country. A first-offense DUI with a BAC of 0.08-0.149% is a Class 1 misdemeanor carrying up to 12 months in jail, a fine up to $2,500, a mandatory $250 minimum fine, and a 12-month license suspension. BAC of 0.15-0.20% triggers mandatory minimum five days in jail. A BAC above 0.20% requires mandatory minimum ten days. We challenge the legality of the traffic stop, field sobriety test administration, breathalyzer calibration records, and blood draw procedures.

Reckless Driving
In Virginia, driving 20 mph or more over the posted speed limit, or driving at any speed exceeding 85 mph, is reckless driving — a Class 1 misdemeanor, not a traffic infraction. This carries up to 12 months in jail, a $2,500 fine, six demerit points, and a criminal record that follows you. Many people are shocked to learn a speeding ticket is actually a criminal charge. We appear in court on your behalf and work to reduce or dismiss the charge.

Misdemeanor Defense
Class 1 and Class 2 misdemeanors include assault and battery, petty larceny (theft under $1,000), trespass, possession of marijuana over the legal limit, and first-offense drug possession. We work to minimize consequences, pursue diversion programs where available, and advise on expungement eligibility.

Felony Defense
Felony charges carry lifelong consequences including loss of voting rights, firearm rights, and professional licenses. We provide representation from arraignment through trial, including bail hearings, motions to suppress evidence, plea negotiations, and jury trial defense.


ESTATE PLANNING

Wills
A valid Virginia will requires the person to be at least 18 years old, of sound mind, and the document must be signed by the testator and witnessed by two competent witnesses who are not beneficiaries. Holographic wills (entirely handwritten and signed) are valid in Virginia without witnesses. Without a valid will, Virginia intestacy laws determine how your assets pass — which often does not match your wishes, especially for blended families or unmarried partners.

Revocable Living Trusts
A revocable trust allows your estate to bypass probate, maintain privacy (probate is public record), and plan for disability. Assets must be formally retitled into the trust name to be governed by it. We handle the trust document and the asset transfer process.

Powers of Attorney
A durable financial power of attorney designates someone to manage your financial affairs if you become incapacitated. A healthcare power of attorney (or advance directive) designates someone to make medical decisions. Without these documents, your family may need to petition the court for a guardianship or conservatorship — a costly and time-consuming process.

Special Needs Trusts
Families with disabled dependents require carefully structured trusts to preserve eligibility for government benefits like Medicaid and SSI while still providing supplemental support. A poorly drafted bequest can inadvertently disqualify a disabled beneficiary from critical government programs.

Estate Planning for Military Families
With Quantico Marine Corps Base nearby, we assist military families with service-specific considerations including Survivor Benefit Plans, Servicemembers Civil Relief Act provisions, and deployment-related planning for powers of attorney and guardianship documents.
